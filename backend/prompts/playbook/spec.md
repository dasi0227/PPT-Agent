## Procedure

1. **Understand the Slide**: Use the current request and relevant references to determine what the audience should understand, remember, or be able to do after this slide. Use `read_resource` and `read_image` when missing source information or visual evidence is needed to make these decisions.
2. **Develop the Content**: Establish the main message and select the explanations, evidence, data, or examples needed to support it. Synthesize the material into concrete content and make the relationships that support the message explicit.
3. **Plan the Elements**: Organize the content into meaningful display units. Decide what each element shows, how it contributes to the message, and which representation best communicates it within design's requirements.
4. **Arrange the Composition**: Establish visual hierarchy, group related elements, and plan a reading order that builds the intended understanding. Describe layout in natural language when spatial relationships matter.
5. **Review and Save**: Confirm that the message, elements, and composition support one another, fit the slide's narrative role, and satisfy applicable content and visual requirements. Save the new or revised spec with `edit_spec`.

## References

- **Manifest**: Use the audience and intended outcome to determine content depth, terminology, and material selection. Apply the required language, content coverage, and exclusions to the slide's contribution.
- **Outline**: Use the slide's title, section and subsection titles, and relevant neighboring slides to establish its narrative role, avoid repetition, and connect its message to the surrounding content.
- **Existing spec**: Use the current existing spec and, when needed, HTML or screenshots to identify which content and composition decisions need revision.
